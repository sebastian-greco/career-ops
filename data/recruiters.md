# Recruiter & Talent-Channel Target List

Built for Sebastian Greco — Director / Head of Eng / Senior EM (selective Staff/Principal in LLM·agentic·crypto), remote-first EU, €110–150K total compensation or €75–100/hr contractor.

**Golden rule:** legitimate executive/contingent recruiters are **paid by the employer, never by you**. If anyone calling themselves a "headhunter" asks you for a placement fee, walk away. Candidate-paid "pay-when-you-land" coaches are a different, riskier category — covered at the bottom.

**Where the market actually went (2024–2026):** Gergely Orosz [shut down his Talent Collective + job board](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/tech-jobs-board/) in 2024. His conclusion: AI-spam has gutted job boards, and hiring for senior+/leadership has shifted to **specialist search firms (employer-paid on success)** and **direct sourcing**. That's exactly why this recruiter-first pivot is the right call.

**Suggested effort mix:** ~40% recruiter channel · ~30% direct/network outreach · ~20% selective online apps · ~10% LinkedIn proof and visibility.

> **Level note:** "Executive search" = **Director / Head / VP+**. Plain **EM / Senior EM** sit below that line → use contingent recruiters, marketplaces (Tier 3), and direct outreach for those.

## Evidence from the 2026 search

- More than 50 online applications produced two interview processes: Pennylane and Sourcegraph. That is an initial-interview conversion of **less than 4%**.
- Two relevant LinkedIn approaches from external recruiters produced Recare and Scalapay. The denominator for recruiter outreach is unknown, so this is not a valid conversion-rate comparison, but both relevant approaches became serious conversations.
- The mailbox contains other recruiter InMails. Most were lower-fit, consulting-heavy, geographically constrained, or did not progress. The problem is therefore **qualified inbound**, not zero visibility.
- Scalapay and Sourcegraph were strong interview processes. Scalapay changed the role to a banking-experienced Director; Sourcegraph gave no candidate-specific rejection reason. Pennylane provides the clearest interview-performance lesson. Recare is paused rather than rejected.

**Operating conclusion:** keep online applications as a discovery and optionality channel, but do not let them consume most search time. Build repeat relationships with recruiters who hold relevant live mandates and add a human path to every high-priority online application.

## How to find the next 15 relevant recruiters

Build a small recruiter graph, not an agency spreadsheet.

1. **Start with mandates, not firms.** Search LinkedIn posts and jobs for `"Senior Engineering Manager"`, `"Director of Engineering"`, `"Head of Engineering"`, `remote Europe`, and the target verticals: SaaS, developer tools, AI, healthtech, and fintech/payments.
2. **Open the people behind each mandate.** Prioritize recruiters whose last 60–90 days show repeated work at Sebastian's level, in Europe, for product companies—not general IT staffing.
3. **Expand from known-good firms.** Review André's and Max's colleagues, former colleagues, commenters, and shared client announcements. Recruiters often move firms while keeping the same specialty and network.
4. **Map target companies backward.** For each high-priority scale-up, identify the internal executive recruiter and any external search partner named in hiring posts. Approach the person who owns the search, not a generic agency inbox.
5. **Qualify before investing.** A useful recruiter can name their geography, seniority band, client type, current or recent mandates, and process. If they cannot, keep them as a passive connection rather than scheduling a long registration call.

- `("engineering leadership" OR "VP Engineering" OR "Head of Engineering") (recruiter OR "executive search") Europe SaaS`

## LinkedIn visibility — what to do before paying

1. Turn on **Open to Work → Recruiters only** and specify the exact target titles, remote preference, and realistic European locations. LinkedIn Recruiter has a dedicated Open-to-Work filter/spotlight.
2. Make the headline searchable and specific. A working version:
   **Engineering Leader | Senior EM / Director | Led 27 engineers across 4 teams | SaaS, Platform & Applied AI | Remote Europe**
3. Ensure the About and recent Riverside role naturally contain the terms recruiters filter for: Engineering Leadership, Senior Engineering Manager, Director of Engineering, manager of managers, platform/backend, SaaS, distributed teams, and applied AI.
4. Use Featured for two or three proof artifacts or posts, not a general content dump.
5. Create light activity: one useful post every two weeks and two or three substantive comments per week in the engineering-leadership/scale-up network. The objective is recruiter recall and evidence of judgment, not becoming a creator.

### Premium decision

LinkedIn Premium Career currently adds five InMails per month, Top Applicant/Top Choice tools, deeper profile-view analytics, and applicant insights. LinkedIn's published feature list does **not** promise that a paid account is automatically ranked higher in Recruiter search.

Use Premium only as a **one-month outbound and measurement experiment**:

- send the five InMails to named, high-fit recruiters or hiring leaders;
- inspect which recruiter titles, companies, searches, and locations produce profile views;
- compare qualified recruiter views, replies, and conversations with the prior four weeks;
- cancel if it creates analytics but no additional relevant conversations.

## Six-week operating cadence

Each week:

- **5 named recruiter contacts:** two warm/follow-up, three new and mandate-qualified;
- **2 direct-network asks:** former executives, peers, investors, or founders who can route to a hiring leader;
- **3–5 selective applications:** only strong-fit roles, each followed by one relevant human contact within 48 hours;
- **2–3 substantive LinkedIn comments;** one original proof-led post every other week;
- **Friday review:** record source, response, recruiter conversation, client submission, first interview, and next action.

Evaluate after six weeks using outcomes, not vanity metrics:

| Metric | What it tells us |
|--------|------------------|
| Qualified recruiter replies / contacts | Whether targeting and positioning are working |
| Recruiter conversations / replies | Whether the pitch earns a real screen |
| Client submissions / recruiter conversations | Whether agencies actually hold suitable mandates |
| First interviews / channel | Which channel deserves more time |
| Profile views from recruiters in target markets | Whether LinkedIn discoverability is improving |

Do not compare the channels only by raw interview count. Track the denominator and the quality of the opportunity.

## Caution — candidate-paid "career coaches"

"Pay-when-you-land" coaches charge **you** (flat fee or % of first-year salary). The contingent-on-landing structure creates pressure to make you accept *anything*. For your profile this is usually not worth it — you have a channel/distribution problem, not a packaging problem. If you ever consider one, demand: named clients, verifiable placement outcomes at your level, fixed (not %-of-salary) pricing, and no exclusivity lock-in.
